AI for Advocacy isn't just about helping people understand government. It's about giving people the knowledge and tools to stand up for what they believe in.

The central idea is that people shouldn't need a law degree, a political science degree, money for a consultant, or connections to powerful people to participate meaningfully in the systems that affect their lives.

Here's how I'd frame the project for Granola and Claude.

# AI for Advocacy

**Understand the system. Find your voice. Make yourself heard.**

## The mission

AI for Advocacy helps people understand the systems that shape their lives and advocate effectively for what they believe in.

Government processes, public policies, regulations, and institutional procedures can be difficult to navigate. Information is scattered, official language can be inaccessible, and many people don't know where to begin when they want to challenge a decision, propose a change, or make their voices heard.

AI for Advocacy bridges that gap.

By combining artificial intelligence with reliable research, primary-source documentation, and practical guidance, the platform helps people understand complex issues, identify who has the authority to address them, explore their options, and take informed action.

## What it helps people do

**Understand:** Translate complicated government documents, policies, regulations, and procedures into accessible language.

**Investigate:** Find relevant records, identify decision-makers, compare official statements with published documents, and determine what additional information is needed.

**Advocate:** Develop arguments, prepare public comments, draft correspondence, submit public records requests, and communicate concerns effectively.

**Organize:** Build advocacy plans, track deadlines, prepare for meetings, document commitments, and coordinate lawful civic participation.

**Follow through:** Monitor decisions, evaluate responses, and identify appropriate next steps.

## Our guiding principle

AI for Advocacy does not tell people what to believe. It helps them understand what they believe, research the issues that matter to them, and communicate their positions effectively.

The platform should serve people across the political spectrum and support advocacy on a wide range of issues. Its role is not to determine which causes deserve support, but to make the tools of informed participation more accessible.

## The vision

A world in which people can participate more effectively in the decisions that affect their lives—not because they have money, influence, or specialized knowledge, but because they have access to information and the tools to use it.

Advocacy should not be reserved for people who already know how the system works.

AI for Advocacy exists to help more people find their footing, make their case, and have a meaningful voice.

## What makes AI for Advocacy different?

I would build the product around a simple idea:

A person brings the issue. The AI helps them figure out what they can do about it.

For example, someone might arrive with a concern about utility bills, housing policy, school funding, environmental protections, workplace rights, public spending, or a decision made by their city council.

AI for Advocacy would help them move through a repeatable process:

1. **Tell your story.** Explain what is happening and why it matters to you.
2. **Define your goal.** What would you like to change, protect, understand, or achieve?
3. **Understand the system.** Identify the relevant institutions, decision-makers, rules, and procedures.
4. **Build your case.** Gather credible sources, understand counterarguments, and distinguish evidence from assumptions.
5. **Choose your strategy.** Explore the available avenues for action and their respective limitations.
6. **Take action.** Create the documents, communications, questions, and plans you need.
7. **Track your progress.** Record responses, deadlines, decisions, and follow-up actions.

The AI should explain uncertainty, link to authoritative sources, and distinguish general information from legal advice. It should never invent regulations, deadlines, or procedural requirements.

And critically, it should help people understand their options rather than push them toward a particular political position.

## What I want you to build first

Rather than starting with a broad platform, I would begin with an interactive Advocacy Assistant.

A user enters a concern in their own words and answers three questions:

- What is happening?
- What outcome would you like?
- Where is this happening?

The tool then creates a personalized advocacy roadmap, including relevant decision-makers, verified sources, potential courses of action, and a first concrete step.

That is a useful, testable foundation you could develop with Claude and expand over time.

Granola could become useful later for turning public meetings, community conversations, and stakeholder discussions into organized notes and follow-up actions. But it doesn't need to be central to the first version.

## One important design principle

AI for Advocacy should increase people's independence, not make them dependent on AI.

Users should be able to inspect the sources, understand the reasoning, revise the proposed strategy, and decide for themselves what to do. The platform should help people develop their own capacity to advocate, not simply generate letters for them to send.

The ultimate goal is to make effective advocacy more accessible to everyone.

And I would keep that mission at the center as you build.
